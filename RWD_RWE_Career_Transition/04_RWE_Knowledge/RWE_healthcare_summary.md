# RWE in Healthcare — Data Analysis Summary

Real-world evidence (RWE) in healthcare refers to insights generated from data collected outside traditional randomized clinical trials. These data sources include electronic health records, insurance claims, patient registries, pharmacy databases, wearable devices, and patient-reported outcomes. A data analysis of RWE is typically conducted to evaluate treatment effectiveness, safety, adherence, healthcare utilization, and clinical outcomes in routine medical practice.

The key advantage of RWE is that it reflects how therapies and interventions perform in real-world settings across broader and more diverse patient populations. It can support treatment comparisons, identify outcomes in specific subgroups, assess long-term impact, and inform regulatory, reimbursement, and clinical decision-making.

However, RWE analysis also presents important challenges. Real-world datasets may contain missing information, inconsistent coding, variable data quality, and potential biases such as confounding and selection bias. As a result, robust study design, transparent methodology, and appropriate statistical adjustment are essential to ensure reliable and meaningful conclusions.

Overall, RWE has become an increasingly important component of modern healthcare research because it complements clinical trial evidence and helps stakeholders understand how healthcare interventions perform in everyday practice.
