# Cover Letter - Final Version
## Gamma Knife Radiosurgery for Cavernous Sinus–Invading Acromegaly

---

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled **"Gamma Knife Radiosurgery for Cavernous Sinus–Invading Acromegaly: An International Multicenter Retrospective Study"** for consideration for publication in [Journal Name].

Cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly represents a clinically distinct and surgically challenging subgroup, in which complete resection is often not feasible and optimal management strategies remain uncertain. Although Gamma Knife radiosurgery (GKS) is widely used in this setting, the determinants of endocrine remission, recurrence, and treatment-related toxicity have not been comprehensively characterized within a unified framework.

In this international multicenter study, we analyzed a large cohort of patients with cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly treated with GKS and integrated clinical, endocrine, radiographic, surgical, and dosimetric variables. Our study provides three key contributions:

**First**, we identify independent predictors of endocrine remission and toxicity, demonstrating that outcomes are driven by a complex interplay of hormonal burden, tumor characteristics, treatment timing, and radiosurgical parameters.

**Second**, using propensity-based overlap weighting, we provide comparative effectiveness insights into clinically relevant treatment strategies, including early versus delayed radiosurgery, targeted versus whole-sellar planning, and peri-radiosurgical medication management.

**Third**, we extend prior work by applying advanced analytical approaches, including multi-state modeling to characterize disease trajectories and internally validated prediction models incorporating multimodal data. These models demonstrate robust performance across centers and provide clinically interpretable tools for individualized risk stratification.

To our knowledge, this is among the first studies to focus specifically on cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly while integrating surgical, radiosurgical, endocrine, and dosimetric factors within a comprehensive analytical framework. The findings have direct implications for treatment sequencing, radiosurgical planning, and patient counseling.

This manuscript has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission and have no conflicts of interest to disclose.

We believe this work will be of interest to clinicians and researchers in neurosurgery, endocrinology, and radiation oncology, as well as those focused on precision medicine and multimodal treatment strategies.
