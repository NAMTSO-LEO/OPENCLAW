### Key Points from NEJM Review

#### Epidemiology
- Incidence: ~10 cases per million
- 20% of pituitary tumors secrete GH
- 95% sporadic (no known genetic cause)
- 25% co-secrete prolactin

#### Diagnostic Delay
- Average delay: >5 years
- 25% have >10 year delay
- Delay correlates with: higher GH at diagnosis, more comorbidities

#### Clinical Presentation
- 70% have macroadenomas at diagnosis
- Physical changes most common presentation (facial features 21.5%, enlarged hands/feet 13.6%)
- Common comorbidities: thyroid enlargement (34%), hypertension (28.8%), diabetes (27.5%), sleep apnea (25.5%)

#### Key Message for Your Study
This review emphasizes:
1. Early diagnosis is critical
2. Multimodal treatment often needed
3. CS invasion makes surgery challenging → need for radiation
4. Long-term follow-up essential

## Key Consolidated Findings

### 1. CS Invasion = Master Variable
- All studies consistently show CS invasion is strongest negative predictor
- Surgery remission: r = -0.953 (Anik)
- GKS remission: HR ~0.34-0.64 (multiple studies)
- Affects both surgery and radiation outcomes

### 2. Time is Central
- Remission is time-dependent, not binary
- Low-dose: 76% at 12 years
- Standard dose: 60-70% at 5-8 years
- Toxicity cumulative over 5-10+ years

### 3. Dose Not Linear
- Simple cutoffs inadequate
- Spline modeling needed
- Isodose line important for toxicity

### 4. Pediatric vs Adult
- Lower remission in pediatric acromegaly (42%)
- Higher CS invasion rate (66.7%)
- Different margin dose (18 Gy median)
