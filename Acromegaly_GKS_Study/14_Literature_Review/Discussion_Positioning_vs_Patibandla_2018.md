## Suggested Discussion Paragraph

### Timing of Remission Following Radiosurgery

Patibandla et al. provided important insights into the temporal pattern of endocrine remission following stereotactic radiosurgery for acromegaly. Their cohort of 157 patients demonstrated that while the median time to remission was 29 months, approximately 40% of patients achieved remission beyond this point, with 8-year actuarial remission reaching 77%.

Several factors were associated with delayed remission. The interval from last surgical resection to radiosurgery was significantly associated with time to remission (HR 1.013 per month, p=0.007), suggesting that earlier intervention when residual disease is identified may lead to more prompt biochemical control. Interestingly, patients with cavernous sinus included in the treatment target achieved earlier remission (54.8% in early vs 32.5% in late group, p=0.041), potentially indicating more aggressive treatment of invasive disease is beneficial.

Our study extends these findings by specifically examining cavernous sinus-invading disease as the primary focus, applying more sophisticated analytical approaches including overlap weighting to address confounding, and characterizing the full time-to-event distribution rather than binary early/late categorization.
