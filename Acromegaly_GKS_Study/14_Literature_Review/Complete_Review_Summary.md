### Key Technical Points

#### Radiobiology

- Single high-dose fraction vs multiple fractions
- Steep dose gradient spares normal tissue
- "Hot spot" at center increases cell kill
- Hypoxic tumor core more radioresistant

#### Medication Effects

- **Critical finding**: Anti-secretory medications (octreotide, etc.) at time of SRS reduce remission rates
- Should stop 8 weeks before SRS
- Resume 6-8 weeks after SRS

#### Imaging Requirements

- Thin-slice MRI (1mm)
- Post-contrast volume acquisition
- CT for bone anatomy
- PET co-registration for functional mapping

### Gamma Knife Planning Principles

1. **Isodose selection**: 50% most common (steepest fall-off)
2. **Beam blocking**: Shield optic apparatus
3. **Frame angle**: Optimize to minimize optic dose
4. **Multiple isocenters**: Conform to tumor shape

## Final Framework for Your Study

### What the Literature Tells Us

1. **CS invasion is the central challenge** - affects surgery, radiation, outcomes
2. **Remission is time-dependent** - 5-year ~45%, 10-year ~60%
3. **Baseline IGF-1 is key predictor** - consistently strong
4. **Dose matters but has limits** - 25 Gy seems optimal
5. **Toxicity is delayed** - 10+ years follow-up needed
6. **Early response predicts** - 6-month GH drop most predictive
7. **Medication matters** - hold before/after SRS

### What Makes Your Study Novel

1. **First dedicated CS-invading cohort** - addresses clear gap
2. **Multicenter international** - exceeds single-center literature
3. **Modern analysis** - spline, weighting, dynamic prediction
4. **Integrated approach** - combines efficacy + safety modeling

## Ready-to-Use Introduction Framework

> "Gamma Knife stereotactic radiosurgery (GKRS) has emerged as an effective adjuvant treatment for acromegaly, with reported endocrine remission rates of 40-60% at 5 years and up to 70% at 10 years. However, the existing literature is limited by heterogeneous patient populations, inconsistent remission definitions, and predominantly single-center data. In particular, patients with cavernous sinus-invading tumors - the most challenging surgical subset - remain poorly characterized. We performed a multicenter international retrospective study to address these limitations and provide contemporary outcome data for this important subpopulation."
