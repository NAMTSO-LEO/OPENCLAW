## Structured, Journal-Ready Version

### Background

Cavernous sinus invasion in acromegaly significantly reduces surgical remission rates and poses substantial treatment challenges. Gamma Knife radiosurgery (GKS) is widely used for residual or recurrent disease; however, predictors of durable endocrine remission in this anatomically complex subgroup remain poorly defined.

---

### Objective

To evaluate endocrine and radiographic outcomes after GKS for cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly and to identify clinical, biochemical, and dosimetric predictors of durable remission using conventional and machine learning–augmented models.

---

### Methods

This international multicenter retrospective cohort study included patients with acromegaly and radiographic or treatment-based evidence of cavernous sinus invasion who underwent GKS with at least 12 months of follow-up. The primary endpoint was **durable endocrine remission**, defined as normalization of IGF-1 off medical therapy without recurrence. Secondary endpoints included time to remission, radiographic control, biochemical recurrence, new hypopituitarism, and need for salvage therapy.

Time-to-event outcomes were analyzed using **Kaplan–Meier methods** and **Cox proportional hazards models**, while binary outcomes were evaluated using **logistic regression**. Penalized regression (LASSO/elastic net) was applied for variable selection. Machine learning models, including random forest, gradient boosting, and random survival forest, were developed to enhance predictive performance. Internal-external validation across centers was performed. Model performance was assessed using discrimination, calibration, and decision curve analysis.

### Conclusion

Gamma Knife radiosurgery provides **excellent tumor control and clinically meaningful endocrine remission** in cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly. Endocrine outcomes are strongly influenced by **baseline hormonal burden, tumor characteristics, treatment timing, and radiosurgical dosimetry**. Integrating clinical and dosimetric factors using predictive modeling may enable individualized treatment strategies in this challenging population.

## Short Abstract

Gamma Knife radiosurgery (GKS) is commonly used for cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly, yet predictors of durable endocrine remission remain unclear. In this international multicenter study, we evaluated outcomes and predictive factors following GKS.

Durable remission, defined as normalization of IGF-1 off therapy without recurrence, was achieved in a subset of patients and occurred over a prolonged time course. Lower IGF-1 index, smaller tumor volume, earlier radiosurgery, medication hold, and higher biologically effective dose were associated with improved outcomes. Radiographic tumor control was high, while hypopituitarism remained the most common adverse effect.

Machine learning models demonstrated improved predictive performance and identified key contributors to remission, including IGF-1 index and dosimetric parameters. These findings support the role of GKS in cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly and highlight factors that may guide individualized treatment planning.
