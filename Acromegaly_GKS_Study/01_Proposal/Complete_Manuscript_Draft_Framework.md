## TITLE

Gamma Knife Radiosurgery for Cavernous Sinus–Invading Acromegaly: Predictors of Durable Remission, Toxicity, and Dynamic Treatment Response in an International Multicenter Cohort

---

## ABSTRACT

### Background
Acromegaly caused by growth hormone–secreting pituitary adenomas is associated with significant morbidity if inadequately controlled. While transsphenoidal surgery remains first-line therapy, biochemical remission declines substantially in the presence of cavernous sinus invasion. Gamma Knife radiosurgery (GKRS) is widely used for residual or recurrent disease, yet current evidence is largely derived from heterogeneous cohorts and lacks focused analyses of cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly.

### Objective
To evaluate durable endocrine remission, recurrence, and treatment-related toxicity following GKRS in cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly, and to identify clinical, biochemical, and dosimetric predictors of long-term outcomes.

### Methods
We conducted an international multicenter retrospective cohort study of patients with cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly treated with GKRS. The primary endpoint was durable endocrine remission, defined as normalization of IGF-1 off medical therapy without recurrence at last follow-up. Secondary endpoints included endocrine control, time to remission, recurrence, salvage therapy, radiographic control, and hypopituitarism. Kaplan–Meier and Cox proportional hazards models were used, with restricted cubic spline modeling for continuous predictors. Comparative analyses for treatment strategies were performed using propensity weighting.

## INTRODUCTION

### Paragraph 1 — Disease + surgical limitation
Acromegaly is a chronic endocrine disorder caused by growth hormone–secreting pituitary adenomas and is associated with substantial cardiovascular, metabolic, and neuroendocrine morbidity. Transsphenoidal surgery remains the standard first-line treatment; however, biochemical remission rates decline significantly when tumors invade the cavernous sinus, as such invasion increases surgical complexity and limits the feasibility of gross-total resection.

### Paragraph 2 — Role of radiosurgery
Gamma Knife radiosurgery (GKRS) has emerged as an effective adjunctive or salvage therapy for persistent or recurrent acromegaly, particularly when residual tumor is confined to the cavernous sinus. Prior studies and meta-analyses have demonstrated excellent radiographic tumor control and moderate endocrine remission rates, typically occurring over several years rather than immediately after treatment.

### Paragraph 3 — Fragmented literature
Despite these advances, the current literature remains fragmented. Multicenter studies have identified key predictors such as baseline IGF-1 burden, medication status, and treatment timing, while dosimetric analyses have highlighted the importance of biologically effective dose (BED). Additionally, comparative studies suggest that radiosurgical planning strategies may influence toxicity more than efficacy. However, these findings are largely derived from mixed acromegaly populations, and no unified analysis has focused specifically on cavernous sinus–invading disease.

### Paragraph 4 — Gap + aim
Therefore, we conducted an international multicenter retrospective study specifically focused on cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly to evaluate the long-term endocrine and safety outcomes after GKRS, and to determine how baseline endocrine burden, treatment timing, medication status, and radiosurgical planning influence the dynamic trajectory of remission, recurrence, and toxicity.

## METHODS

### Study Design and Patients
We performed a multicenter retrospective cohort study across [X] participating institutions. Consecutive patients with biochemically confirmed acromegaly and radiographic evidence of cavernous sinus invasion or intracavernous residual tumor who underwent Gamma Knife radiosurgery were eligible. Patients were included if they had at least [X] months of endocrine and imaging follow-up after GKRS. The study was approved by the institutional review board at each participating center, with informed consent waived due to the retrospective nature.

### Data Collection
Clinical, biochemical, radiographic, and treatment data were collected from electronic medical records using a standardized case report form. Key variables included demographic information, preoperative and preoperative endocrine profiles, tumor characteristics, surgical history, GKRS treatment parameters, and follow-up outcomes.

### Exposure Variables (Four Domains)

**Biology:**
- Baseline IGF-1 and IGF-1 index (IGF-1 / age-sex-adjusted upper limit of normal)
- Baseline random GH
- OGTT nadir GH when available

**Anatomy:**
- Knosp grade
- Residual tumor volume (cm³)
- Cavernous sinus laterality and extent

**Treatment State:**
- Surgery-to-GKRS interval (months)
- Postoperative residual vs recurrent disease at time of GKRS
- Medication status at GKRS (held vs continued; types of medications)
- Prior medical therapy

**Radiation/Planning:**
- Margin dose (Gy)
- Maximum dose (Gy)
- Isodose line (%)
- Maximum point dose to optic apparatus (Gy)
- Treatment planning approach (whole-sella vs targeted)
- Biologically effective dose (BED) when calculable

### Endpoints

**Primary Endpoint:**
- **Durable endocrine remission:** Age- and sex-adjusted IGF-1 normalization off all GH/IGF-1-lowering medications, confirmed at last follow-up, with supportive OGTT nadir GH <1 ng/mL where available, and without biochemical recurrence

**Secondary Endpoints:**
- Endocrine control (IGF-1 normalization on medication)
- Time to endocrine remission
- Radiographic tumor control (stable or decreased tumor volume)
- Biochemical recurrence after initial remission
- Salvage therapy (repeat GKRS, additional surgery, fractionated RT, or medical escalation)
- New hypopituitarism (any new pituitary hormone deficiency)
- Visual or cranial nerve toxicity
- Overall survival

### Statistical Analysis
Baseline characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics. Time-to-event outcomes were analyzed using Kaplan–Meier survival curves, with comparisons between groups performed by the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards regression was used to identify predictors of time-to-event outcomes, with results reported as hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

**Continuous predictors** (IGF-1i, surgery-to-GKRS interval, tumor volume, BED) were modeled using restricted cubic splines to flexibly characterize nonlinear relationships with outcomes. Multivariable models were built using backward selection, with covariates retained at P<0.10.

**Comparative effectiveness** of treatment strategies (whole-sella vs targeted planning; medication hold vs continuation) was assessed using inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) or propensity score matching, with balance diagnostics evaluated using standardized mean differences.

**Sensitivity analyses** were performed to assess robustness to unmeasured confounding (E-value), to missing data (multiple imputation where appropriate), and to alternative endpoint definitions.

All statistical tests were two-sided, with P<0.05 considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed using SAS version [X] and R version [X].

## DISCUSSION

### Paragraph 1: Main Findings
In this international multicenter cohort of cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly, GKRS provided substantial long-term endocrine and radiographic control. The durable remission rate was [X]% at [X] years, with a median time to remission of [X] months. Key predictors of remission included [list key predictors]. New hypopituitarism occurred in [X]% of patients, with a median time to onset of [X] months.

### Paragraph 2: Comparison with Prior Studies
Our findings are consistent with prior multicenter analyses from the International Gamma Knife Research Foundation, which identified medication status as a predictor of durable remission, and with Mayo Clinic analyses demonstrating that IGF-1 index and biologically effective dose more reliably predict outcomes than physical dose alone. Additionally, our toxicity findings align with prior series showing that hypopituitarism is the most common delayed adverse event after pituitary radiosurgery.

### Paragraph 3: Why This Study Is Stronger
Unlike prior studies that included heterogeneous acromegaly populations, we specifically focused on cavernous sinus–invading disease—the subgroup in which surgical cure is least likely and adjuvant radiotherapy decision-making is most consequential. This focused design provides clinically relevant data for the population most commonly referred for GKRS.

### Paragraph 4: Clinical Implications
These findings have important implications for clinical decision-making. First, early referral for GKRS should be considered when residual cavernous sinus disease is documented after surgery. Second, temporary cessation of IGF-1-lowering medications before GKRS may improve remission rates when clinically feasible. Third, targeted planning strategies may offer comparable efficacy with potentially lower toxicity compared to whole-sella approaches. Finally, baseline endocrine burden and dosimetric parameters should be used to individualize patient expectations.

### Paragraph 5: Limitations
Several limitations warrant consideration. This was a retrospective study subject to selection bias and incomplete data. Endocrine assays and follow-up protocols varied across centers. Biologically effective dose could not be calculated for all patients due to missing treatment time data. Additionally, treatment decisions (e.g., medication hold, planning strategy) were not randomized, limiting causal inferences. Despite these limitations, our focused multicenter design addresses a major gap in the existing literature on radiosurgery for cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly.

---

## CONCLUSIONS

Gamma Knife radiosurgery is an effective long-term treatment for cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly, but its success is best understood as a dynamic process rather than a binary endpoint. Durable remission reflects the interplay between endocrine burden, treatment timing, medication state, and radiosurgical planning, whereas delayed hypopituitarism remains the principal tradeoff. These findings support individualized treatment strategies that consider baseline biology, anatomical constraints, and radiosurgical approach to optimize long-term outcomes.
