- Study design + patients

#### Results (3-4 paragraphs)
- Patient characteristics (1 paragraph)
- Outcomes (1-2 paragraphs)

- Principal findings

## Sample Short Report Text

### Introduction

Cavernous sinus–invading pituitary adenomas represent a surgically challenging subgroup in acromegaly, with lower remission rates after transsphenoidal surgery. Gamma Knife stereotactic radiosurgery (GKS) is widely used as adjuvant treatment, but predictors of long-term endocrine outcomes in this specific population remain poorly characterized. We conducted an international multicenter study to evaluate endocrine remission rates, predictors, and treatment-related toxicity after GKS for cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly.

### Methods

We reviewed records from X centers of patients with cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly treated with GKS. Endocrine remission was defined as IGF-1 index ≤1.0 with no ongoing medical therapy. Predictors were evaluated using Cox proportional hazards regression. Propensity score overlap weighting was applied to balance treatment groups.

### Results

We included N patients with median follow-up of X months. Cumulative remission was X% at 3 years and X% at 5 years. Lower baseline IGF-1 index (HR X.X, 95% CI X.X–X.X), higher biologically effective dose (HR X.X), and shorter surgery-to-GKS interval (HR X.X) were independently associated with remission. New hypopituitarism occurred in X% of patients. A prediction model achieved C-index of X.X.

### Discussion

GKS achieves meaningful endocrine remission in cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly, with outcomes influenced by baseline hormonal burden, dosimetry, and treatment timing. Earlier radiosurgery and optimized dosing may improve outcomes. A validated prediction model can support individualized counseling.
