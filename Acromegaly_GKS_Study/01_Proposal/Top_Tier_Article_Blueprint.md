# Top-Tier Article Blueprint
## Gamma Knife Radiosurgery for Cavernous Sinus–Invading Acromegaly

---

## Title

**Primary:**
Gamma Knife Radiosurgery for Cavernous Sinus–Invading Acromegaly: An International Multicenter Study of Durable Remission, Toxicity, and Dynamic Treatment Response

**Alternative (more conservative):**
Gamma Knife Radiosurgery for Cavernous Sinus–Invading Acromegaly: Predictors of Durable Endocrine Remission, Recurrence, and Hypopituitarism in an International Multicenter Cohort

---

## Core Statement

> In cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly, outcomes after GKRS are determined by the interaction between tumor biology, treatment timing, medication state, and radiosurgical planning, with remission and toxicity evolving over time.

---

## Abstract Structure

### Background
- Surgery is first-line for acromegaly, but remission declines substantially with cavernous sinus invasion
- GKRS widely used for persistent/recurrent disease
- Current evidence fragmented, based on mixed acromegaly cohorts, lacks focused CS-invading analysis

### Objective
- Evaluate durable endocrine remission, recurrence, salvage therapy, and hypopituitarism after GKRS for CS-invading acromegaly
- Identify clinical, biochemical, anatomical, and dosimetric predictors of long-term outcomes

### Methods
- International multicenter retrospective cohort
- Inclusion: acromegaly + objective CS invasion/intracavernous residual + GKRS + endocrine/imaging follow-up
- Primary outcome: durable endocrine remission
- Secondary outcomes: endocrine control, time to remission, radiographic control, recurrence, salvage treatment, hypopituitarism, visual/CN toxicity
- Statistics: KM, Cox, multivariable modeling, weighted comparative analyses, spline modeling

### Results
1. Cohort characteristics
2. Durable remission rates
3. Recurrence/salvage
4. Toxicity profile
5. Key predictors

### Conclusions
- GKRS provides durable control for substantial proportion of CS-invading acromegaly
- Long-term outcome reflects dynamic balance between endocrine efficacy and delayed toxicity
- IGF-1 burden, treatment timing, medication status, and radiosurgical planning should guide individualized decision-making

## Introduction: 4-Paragraph Structure

### Paragraph 1: Disease burden + surgery limits
- Acromegaly morbidity and impact
- Surgery as first-line treatment
- Cavernous sinus invasion significantly reduces biochemical remission
- Gross-total resection difficulty increases with CS invasion

### Paragraph 2: Role of GKRS
- GKRS widely used for persistent/recurrent acromegaly
- Literature shows: high tumor control, slower/m heterogeneous endocrine remission
- Hypopituitarism as primary long-term toxicity

### Paragraph 3: Fragmented literature
- IGKRF: medication hold predicts durable remission
- Mayo: IGF-1i and BED better predictors than simple dose
- Whole-sella vs targeted: planning affects toxicity more than efficacy
- BUT: these studies based on mixed acromegaly, not dedicated CS-invading subgroup

### Paragraph 4: Gap + Study Aim
> No international multicenter study has specifically integrated endocrine burden, cavernous sinus anatomy, treatment timing, peri-radiosurgical medication status, and advanced dosimetry in a dedicated cohort of cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly.

## Methods: Enhanced Framework

### 1. Study Design
- International multicenter retrospective cohort
- Consecutive GKRS-treated patients with CS-invading acromegaly
- Centers, years, IRB approval

### 2. Eligibility Criteria
- Clinical and biochemical diagnosis of acromegaly
- Radiographic evidence of CS invasion or targeted intracavernous residual
- Postoperative adjuvant or salvage GKRS (primary only if surgery contraindicated/declined)
- Minimum endocrine follow-up
- At least one endocrine and one imaging assessment after GKRS

### 3. Exposure Framework (Four Domains)

**Biology:**
- Baseline IGF-1i (IGF-1 / age-sex-adjusted ULN)
- Baseline random GH
- OGTT nadir GH when available

**Anatomy:**
- Knosp grade
- Residual tumor volume
- Cavernous sinus laterality/location if available

**Treatment State:**
- Surgery-to-GKRS interval
- Postoperative residual vs recurrent disease
- Medication hold vs no hold (temporary cessation 4-8 weeks before GKRS)
- Prior medical therapy class

**Radiation/Planning:**
- Margin dose (Gy)
- Maximum dose (Gy)
- Isodose line (%)
- Optic maximum dose (Gy)
- Whole-sella vs targeted
- BED when calculable

### 4. Endpoints

**Primary Endpoint:**
- **Durable endocrine remission**
  - Age/sex-adjusted IGF-1 normalization
  - Off GH/IGF-1-lowering medications
  - Supportive OGTT nadir GH where available
  - Maintained without biochemical recurrence through last follow-up

**Key Secondary Endpoints:**
- Endocrine control on medication
- Time to endocrine remission
- Radiographic tumor control
- Biochemical recurrence
- Salvage therapy (repeat SRS, surgery, EBRT, medical escalation)
- New hypopituitarism
- Visual toxicity / CN neuropathy / ARE
- Overall survival

**Exploratory Endpoints:**
- Early vs late remission
- Effect of medication hold
- Dose/BED effects
- Dynamic prediction / multi-state modeling

### 5. Statistical Plan

**Descriptive:**
- Summarize cohort and missingness

**Time-to-event:**
- Kaplan-Meier for durable remission, recurrence-free survival, PFS, time to new hypopituitarism
- Cox proportional hazards for predictors

**Continuous predictors:**
> Continuous variables, including IGF-1i, surgery-to-GKRS interval, tumor volume, and BED, will be modeled using restricted cubic splines to account for nonlinearity.

**Comparative effectiveness:**
- Whole-sella vs targeted
- Medication hold vs no hold
- Early vs delayed GKRS
- Propensity weighting / matching

**Toxicity modeling:**
- Cox for time to new hypopituitarism
- Logistic for rare visual/CN toxicity

**Dynamic modeling:**
- Landmark analysis at 6, 12, 24 months
- Optional multi-state exploratory model:
  - Active disease → Endocrine control → Durable remission → Recurrence / Salvage
  - (with parallel toxicity state)

## Discussion: 5-Paragraph Structure

### Paragraph 1: Main Findings
> In this international multicenter cohort of cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly, GKRS provided substantial long-term endocrine and radiographic control, while outcomes were strongly shaped by baseline endocrine burden, treatment state, and radiosurgical strategy.

### Paragraph 2: Comparison with Key Studies
- IGKRF: medication hold and durable remission
- Mayo: IGF-1i and BED
- Whole-sella paper: planning strategy and toxicity

### Paragraph 3: Why This Study Is Stronger
- Unlike prior mixed acromegaly cohorts
- Focused on CS-invading disease
- Subgroup in whom surgical cure is least likely and radiosurgical decision-making is most consequential

### Paragraph 4: Clinical Implications
- Early referral for GKRS when residual CS disease is evident
- Consider temporary medication withdrawal when safe
- Prioritize targeted planning when feasible
- Use endocrine burden and BED to individualize expectations

### Paragraph 5: Limitations
- Retrospective design
- Heterogeneous assays/follow-up
- Center effects
- Missing OGTT
- Nonrandom treatment allocation
- Despite limitations, focused subgroup design and multicenter scale address major gap in existing radiosurgical literature

### Innovation 2: Integrated Mechanism Framework

### Innovation 3: Dynamic Benefit-Risk View
Durable remission + recurrence + salvage + hypopituitarism as integrated trajectory, not binary yes/no

## Master Conclusion Template

> Gamma Knife radiosurgery is an effective long-term treatment for cavernous sinus–invading acromegaly, but its success is best understood as a dynamic process rather than a binary endpoint. Durable remission reflects the interplay between endocrine burden, treatment timing, medication state, and radiosurgical planning, whereas delayed hypopituitarism remains the principal tradeoff of treatment.
