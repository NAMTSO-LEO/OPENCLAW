## Suggested Discussion Paragraph

### Comparison with Surgical Outcomes

Anik et al. reported one of the largest surgical series (N=401) using the 2010 consensus criteria, demonstrating an overall remission rate of 68.1%, which decreased to only 40.7% in patients with cavernous sinus invasion. Their analysis identified cavernous sinus invasion as the strongest predictor of poor outcome (r = -0.953, p < 0.001), with a very strong correlation between extrapseudocapsular resection and remission (r = 0.810).

Our study provides complementary data on Gamma Knife radiosurgery outcomes in this same difficult-to-treat subgroup. While surgical resection offers the possibility of immediate remission when complete removal is achievable, many patients with cavernous sinus invasion have residual disease requiring additional treatment. Radiosurgery offers a non-invasive alternative with a different risk-benefit profile.
