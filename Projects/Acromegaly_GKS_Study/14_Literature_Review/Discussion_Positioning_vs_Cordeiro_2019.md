## Suggested Discussion Paragraph

### Hypopituitarism After Gamma Knife Radiosurgery

Hypopituitarism is the most common complication following Gamma Knife radiosurgery for pituitary adenomas. The IGKRF multicenter study of 1023 patients provides benchmark data: 24.2% developed new pituitary hormone deficiency, with actuarial rates of 22.4% at 5 years and 31.3% at 10 years. The median time to onset was 39 months.

Importantly, the study identified modifiable risk factors. Lower isodose line was the strongest independent predictor (HR 1.38 per 10% decrease, p=0.001), suggesting that more conformal treatment planning with higher isodose lines may reduce pituitary toxicity. Whole-sellar treatment was also associated with increased risk (HR 1.452, p=0.033).

Our study extends these findings by specifically examining cavernous sinus-invading acromegaly, a population that may require more extensive treatment volumes and thus face potentially higher risks of hypopituitarism.
