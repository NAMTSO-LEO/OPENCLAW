### Study Overview

This is the **largest direct comparison** of fractionated radiotherapy (FRT) vs stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) for acromegaly, using data from the German Acromegaly Registry.

---

### Key Results

#### Patient Population

| Characteristic | FRT (n=233) | SRS (n=119) | P-value |
|----------------|-------------|-------------|---------|
| Female | 54% | 50% | 0.52 |
| Mean age at diagnosis | 40.7 years | 39.4 years | 0.30 |
| Mean follow-up | 13.0 years | 8.9 years | <0.001 |
| GH before RT (median) | 6.3 ng/mL | 3.5 ng/mL | <0.001 |

#### Remission Rates

| Outcome | FRT | SRS | P-value |
|---------|-----|-----|---------|
| **10-year remission** | 48% | 52% | 0.74 |
| **10-year controlled disease** | 23% | 26% | - |
| **At final visit: controlled/remission** | 70% | - | - |
| Mean time in uncontrolled state | 3.0 years | 2.1 years | **0.021** |

#### Transition States (Multi-State Model)

| Time after RT | FRT (Uncontrolled/Controlled/Remission) | SRS |
|---------------|----------------------------------------|-----|
| Median 1.9 years | 60% / 7% / 33% | 50% / 13% / 37% |

#### Hypopituitarism

| Outcome | FRT | SRS | P-value |
|---------|-----|-----|---------|
| **New ACTH/TSH insufficiency** | Higher | OR = 0.54 | **0.049** |
| Final: any insufficiency | 80% | 63% | - |

---

### Key Findings

#### 1. FRT vs SRS: No Significant Difference in Efficacy

> "The 10-year calculated remission rate was 48% for FRT and 52% for SRS (95% CI for the difference is −18 to 26% age points, P = 0.74)"

**Conclusion:** Both modalities achieve similar long-term biochemical control.

#### 2. SRS Achieves Control Faster

- Mean time in uncontrolled state: 2.1 years (SRS) vs 3.0 years (FRT), p=0.021
- SRS has faster onset of biochemical effect

#### 3. SRS Has Lower Hypopituitarism Risk

> "The odds ratio for adrenocorticotropic or thyreotropic insufficiency was 0.54 (95% CI: 0.30–1.00, P = 0.049) in SRS compared to FRT patients"

#### 4. Pre-RT GH Predicts Early Remission

- Patients with remission within 2 years had pre-therapy GH levels 2.9x lower (p=0.014)
- GH levels did not predict ultimate remission, but predicted speed

### Limitations

- Non-randomized (treatment choice by physician)
- Different follow-up durations (FRT longer)
- GH values not standardized over time
- Pre-RT GH different between groups (confounding)
